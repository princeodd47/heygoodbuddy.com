# Tips

## Combat

### Stagger is the whole game

- Every enemy has a **stagger gauge** under its HP bar. Draining it to zero
  makes the enemy **lose its next turn** and **take extra damage**.
- Hitting a **weakness triples** the stagger an attack deals. Hitting a
  resistance does next to nothing, and the gauge refills if you leave it.
- Before you confirm an attack, the game previews how much stagger it will
  do. Check it.
- Spells like Fire, Blizzard, Thunder, Water and Banish have **17** stagger
  power. Surges (Fire Surge, Water Surge and so on) have **25**.
- **Tronn** and the **Warrior of Light** carry **Stagger Power +30%**, which
  boosts everything the wearer does.
- Enemies above your level are harder to stagger. If the gauge barely moves,
  you're probably under-leveled.
- Some enemies, like the **Minituar**, dodge most attacks until staggered.
  Stagger first, then hit them.

### Weaknesses

- Weaknesses stay hidden until you hit one or cast **Libra**. **Leah** and
  **Cloud** both give Libra, and it's cheap. Cast it on anything new.
- Your party's own elements are limited. Rain has Fire, Lasswell has Ice and
  Fina has Light. For **Lightning, Water and Wind**, lean on Visions.
- Look for elemental **scrolls** in chests. They tend to show up just before
  a boss that's weak to that element.

### Bonus Phase, Sweeping Stagger and Resonance

- Whoever staggers an enemy gets an **extra action** at the end of the round.
- Stagger **every enemy in the same turn** for a **Sweeping Stagger**: the
  whole party gets an extra action, and you can fire **one Resonance** at the
  end of it.
- Resonance is a finisher. Save it for this window instead of opening with
  it.
- "All enemies" spells hit each target for less, but if the whole group
  shares a weakness, they're the fastest way to a Sweeping Stagger. On a
  single tough enemy, use single-target spells.

### The timeline

- The strip along the top shows turn order, left to right. Allies are blue,
  enemies red. There's no time pressure, so read it every turn.
- Heal or buff **before** a dangerous enemy's turn comes up, not after.
- An enemy that **glows red** is about to use something devastating. Stagger
  it before its turn, or have everyone **Defend**.

### Limit Bursts

- The purple gauge fills when a character **staggers an enemy** or **takes
  damage**. A stagger-focused party fills it faster.
- Limit Bursts always hit, which matters against evasive enemies.

### Status effects

- **Esuna** and items like **Remedy** cure ailments (Poison, Silence and so
  on), but **not debuffs** like Deprotect or Slow. Wait those out, or cast
  the opposite buff (Protect cancels Deprotect, Haste cancels Slow).
- A physical hit wakes up a **sleeping** or **confused** ally.
- Accessories with **Nullify** effects block a status outright. Leah's
  Nullify Charm and Monk's Prayer Beads both stop Charm.

### Espers

- Summoning is Fina's job and costs 50 MP. The Esper stays 3 turns and ends
  with its finishing move. See [Espers](espers.md).
- Use them in boss fights, ideally lined up with a stagger, rather than
  saving them for an emergency that never comes.

## Visions

- **Mastery is per character.** Each character has their own Mastery Rank
  with each Vision. Moving Cloud from Lasswell to Fina starts Fina at zero,
  but Lasswell's ranks are still there when Cloud goes back to Lasswell.
- MRP goes to the **equipped** Vision, so equip the one you want to rank up
  before the fight.
- **Stat gains and Mastery passives stay with the character** after you swap
  Visions. Rotating Visions early collects passives from several of them.
- Before you swap off a Vision, check whether the character is one or two
  ranks from a passive worth having.
- A Vision's own abilities work as soon as it's equipped. **AP** is the
  budget for the extra learned abilities you equip on top. AP only comes from
  Mastery Ranks: ranks **4** and **9** each give **+8 AP**.
- Match roles where you can (Commando, Breaker, Sentinel, Healer). Rain and
  the Warrior of Light are both Sentinels.
- Magicite is limited, so spend it awakening the Visions you actually use.

## Exploring and saving

- You can **save anywhere on the world map**, plus at save points in towns
  and dungeons. There's also an **Autosave** switch under Options → Gameplay.
- **Tents** fully restore the party's HP and MP. Use one at a save point or
  on the world map. Keep a couple for the save point before a boss.
- **Fast travel** from the Map screen to anywhere you've been. It doesn't
  work underground.
- The **encounter sensor** on the mini-map turns redder as a fight gets
  close. Use it to time saves and heals.
- Dungeons show a **recommended level** when you enter. A couple of levels
  under is fine. Further under, go grind or do side quests first.
- Ride a **Chocobo** on Grandport's roads to avoid the Antlion ambush.
- Check the weapon and armor shops in every new town, but compare with what
  you've found first. Chests on the way to a town often hold better weapons.
  Sell old gear if you're short.
- Talk to everyone and go into every house. Hidden chests and side quests
  are everywhere.
- **Nothing is permanently missable.** The game warns you before a point of
  no return, and everything behind it can still be done later.
- Battle speed has three settings (the triangles at the bottom left) and can
  be changed mid-fight.

## Difficulty and battle rewards

- Difficulty can be changed **anytime** from the menu.
- **Casual** gives enemies 60% of their HP and a much smaller stagger gauge.
  EXP, gil and drop rates are **the same as Normal**.
- **Expert** is tougher but gives **1.5× MRP**. EXP and gil don't change.
- Bonuses on the results screen:

| Bonus | How | Reward |
|-------|-----|--------|
| **No Damage** | Win without taking any damage | +20% EXP |
| **Overkill** | Finish the fight during the Bonus Phase and keep attacking | +30% gil |
| **Stagger Finish** | Defeat the last enemy while it's staggered | +30% MRP |

- **Everyone gets EXP**, including characters on the bench.
- Running away earns nothing.
- Each enemy rolls separately for its drops: about 1 in 8 or 1 in 12 for the
  normal item, and **1 in 30** for the rare one.

## Farming spots

| What | Where | Why |
|------|-------|-----|
| **EXP, gil** | Grandport world map, near Marauder Cave and the Sanctum of Cloud | **Dhorme Chimera**: 300 EXP and 600 gil each. Weak to Water. |
| **MRP** | Grandport world map | **Minituar**: weak to Ice and Water. Stagger it first or it dodges everything. |
| **Ethers** | Siren's Tower | Best early spot for Ether drops. |
| **EXP** | Dirnado world map, southwest of the Dwarven Forge | **Metal Minituar** in about 3 of 10 fights: **2,500 EXP**. |
| **Gil** | Olderion world map, south | **Mini Gil Snapper**: 1,500 gil. Weak to Ice. Steal from it first. |
| **Laserblade** | Dirnado world map, Mobliz Shipyard | Rare drop from **Centipede**. |
| **Bronze Hammer** | Wind Shrine | Rare drop from **Headstone**. |

### Metal Minituar

- It has only **6 HP**, and every hit that lands does **1 damage**. Normal
  attacks land less than half the time.
- From its fourth turn on, it has about a 1 in 3 chance each turn to run.
- **Multi-hit** abilities roll to hit on every hit, so they're your best bet.
  **Limit Bursts** and **Resonances** always hit.

### Mini Gil Snapper

- It counters physical attacks, so stagger it with **Ice magic**.
- **Zidane** can steal a **Mist Ether**, or rarely a **Small Golden Shell**,
  which sells for 2,500 gil.
