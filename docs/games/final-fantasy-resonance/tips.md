# Tips

## Quick settings

- **Sprint:** Options lets you change sprint from *Hold* to *Toggle* (press
  once to switch) or *Invert* (always run unless you hold the button).
  Running raises the encounter rate.
- **Battle speed:** Press **Triangle** in battle to speed up animations.
  Some Sweeping Staggers reset it, so tap it again. There are three speeds,
  shown by the triangles at the bottom left.
- **Autosave** is a switch under Options → Gameplay.
- **Mini-map:** Click **R3** to hide or show it.
- **Active Time Chats:** If you walk away from one, press **L1** for the list
  and replay it.
- **Auto-cure** in the main menu heals the party with Cure. It spends MP, so
  don't use it to top off small scratches.
- **Help** in the system menu has every tutorial, including what each status
  effect does. Handy mid-boss when something odd hits you.

## Combat

### Stagger is the whole game

- Every enemy has a **stagger gauge** under its HP bar. Draining it to zero
  makes the enemy **lose its next turn** and **take extra damage**.
- Hitting a **weakness triples** the stagger an attack deals. Hitting a
  resistance does next to nothing, and the gauge refills if you leave it.
- Before you confirm an attack, the game shows its damage (red) and stagger
  (yellow). Check the yellow bar.
- Spells like Fire, Blizzard, Thunder, Water and Banish have **17** stagger
  power. Surges (Fire Surge, Water Surge and so on) have **25**.
- **Tronn** and the **Warrior of Light** carry **Stagger Power +30%**, which
  boosts everything the wearer does. The **Stagger Ring** from the Tablet
  Shard boxes adds 10%.
- Enemies above your level are harder to stagger. If the gauge barely moves,
  you're probably under-leveled.
- Some enemies, like the **Minituar**, dodge most attacks until staggered.
  Stagger first, then hit them.
- Save your big hits and buffs for when the enemy is staggered.

### Timing the stagger

- The strip along the top shows turn order, left to right. Allies are blue,
  enemies red. There's no time pressure, so read it every turn.
- The **second** strip shows the order for the **next** round. Use it to plan
  a heal or a break a round ahead.
- Break the enemy that acts **soonest** first. Staggering one that already
  moved this round, or acts last, wastes most of the effect.
- **Counters count toward stagger.** A counter that breaks an enemy after it
  has already acted wastes the break, because stagger wears off at the end
  of the turn.
- Plan who chips the gauge and who breaks it, so the characters after them
  can pile on.
- An enemy that **glows red** is about to use something devastating. Stagger
  it before its turn, or **Defend**. Defending also lets you pass a turn to
  line up a break.
- Heal or buff **before** a dangerous enemy's turn, not after.

### Weaknesses and Libra

- Weaknesses stay hidden until you hit one or cast **Libra**. **Nichol**
  has it, and the **Leah** and **Cloud** Visions give it to anyone.
- Libra shows HP, weaknesses, resistances, traits, status vulnerabilities and
  what you can steal or win. It only needs doing once per enemy type; the
  info stays.
- Some enemies **absorb** an element instead of resisting it.
- Elemental attacks can be **physical or magical**. Rain hits hardest with
  physical moves, Fina and Nichol with magic, and Lasswell and Lid handle
  both. In
  Equipment & Abilities, press **Square** on a Vision to see which of its
  moves are which.
- Your party's own elements are limited. Rain has Fire, Lasswell Ice, Fina
  Light, Lid Earth and Nichol Water. For **Lightning and Wind**, lean on
  Visions.
- Look for elemental **scrolls** in chests. They tend to show up just before
  a boss that's weak to that element.
- If you give your party **Reflect**, it bounces your own healing spells to
  the enemy.

### Bonus Phase, Sweeping Stagger and Resonance

- Whoever staggers an enemy gets an **extra action** at the end of the round.
- Stagger **every enemy in the same turn** for a **Sweeping Stagger**: the
  whole party gets an extra action, and you can fire **one Resonance** at the
  end of it. A lone enemy counts.
- Resonance is a finisher. Save it for this window instead of opening with
  it.
- **Read what each Resonance does.** Many have a side effect: the Warrior of
  Light's buffs the party and Leah's heals everyone and adds Regen. Pick the
  one that fixes your current problem.
- "All enemies" spells hit each target for less, but if the whole group
  shares a weakness, they're the fastest way to a Sweeping Stagger. On a
  single tough enemy, use single-target spells. To target everyone, keep
  moving past the single targets until the whole group lights up.
- When several enemies are close to breaking, spread your attacks so they all
  break in the same turn.

### Limit Bursts

- The purple gauge fills when a character **staggers an enemy** or **takes
  damage**. A stagger-focused party fills it faster.
- **The gauge empties after every battle.** If it's full, use it.
- Limit Bursts always hit, which matters against evasive enemies.

### Status effects

- **Esuna** and items like **Remedy** cure ailments (Poison, Silence and so
  on), but **not debuffs** like Deprotect or Slow. Wait those out, or cast
  the opposite buff (Protect cancels Deprotect, Haste cancels Slow).
- A physical hit wakes up a **sleeping** or **confused** ally.
- Don't cure on reflex. A poisoned ally on low HP needs healing more than an
  Antidote.
- Accessories with **Nullify** effects block a status outright. Leah's
  Nullify Charm and Monk's Prayer Beads both stop Charm.

### Espers

- Summoning is Fina's job and costs 50 MP. The Esper stays 3 turns and ends
  with its finishing move. See [Espers](espers.md).
- Use them in boss fights, ideally lined up with a stagger, rather than
  saving them for an emergency that never comes.
- The finishing move doesn't miss, so it's a good way to land the last hit on
  an evasive enemy.

## Visions

- **Mastery is per character.** Each character has their own Mastery Rank
  with each Vision. Moving Cloud from Lasswell to Fina starts Fina at zero,
  but Lasswell's ranks are still there when Cloud goes back to Lasswell.
- MRP goes to the **equipped** Vision, so equip the one you want to rank up
  before the fight.
- **Stat gains and Mastery passives stay with the character** after you swap
  Visions.
- **Get every Vision to rank 1 or 2 on everyone.** It's quick, and the early
  passives add up. Several restore MP: **Tronn** (5% when you stagger),
  **Warrior of Light** (10% when you stagger) and **Cloud** (3% on a
  critical).
- **AP** comes only from Mastery Ranks: ranks **4** and **9** each give
  **+8 AP**. That's per character, per Vision, so rank 4 on every Vision on
  everyone adds a lot of AP.
- A Vision's own abilities work as soon as it's equipped. AP is the budget
  for the learned abilities you equip on top, from any Vision that
  character has learned them from.
- Match roles where you can (Commando, Breaker, Sentinel, Medic and so on),
  or use a Vision to cover a role the character lacks. The Warrior of Light
  on Lasswell gives him a taunt and healing.
- Don't double up. Fina already heals, so a healing Vision on her is mostly
  overlap. Give healing to someone else and you'll have two healers.
- Before you swap off a Vision, check whether the character is one or two
  ranks from a passive worth having.
- Magicite is limited, so spend it awakening the Visions you actually use.
  Awakened abilities only work while that Vision is equipped. Mastery
  abilities can be equipped on anyone who has learned them.

### Mastery abilities worth getting early

| Vision | Ability | Why |
|--------|---------|-----|
| **Zidane** | **Steal** | Put it on everyone. See [Stealing](#stealing). |
| **Firion** | **Blade Blitz** (rank 5) | A normal attack on every enemy for 0 MP, with decent stagger. Breaks trash fights wide open. |
| **Squall** | **Trigger** | +30% stagger and damage on normal attacks and 0-MP battle skills, Steal included. Pairs with Blade Blitz. |
| **Tidus** | **Reckless Abandonment** | Doubles the stagger of your next physical attack without using your turn. That skill costs 50% more MP. Huge on bosses. |
| **Tidus** | **First Strike** | You act first when a battle starts, so buffs go up before the enemy moves. |
| **Tidus** | **Tip-Top Shape** | Physical damage goes up with each buff on you. |
| **Terra** | **Sharp Mind** (rank 7) | +100% damage when an all-enemies attack hits a single enemy. Stacks with Magic Boost. |
| **Leah** | **Pass the Torch** (rank 5) | Skip your action and hand the turn to an ally, such as your hardest hitter. |
| **Warrior of Light** | **Multi-Cover** + **Physical Counter** | On Rain, he takes every hit from an attack that targets the whole party, and counters once for each ally he covered. |

## Stealing

- **Libra** shows what an enemy has to steal, common and rare.
- If an enemy has only a rare steal, any successful steal gets it. If it has
  two, a successful steal can land on either one, so the rare is harder to
  get.
- Steal before you finish the fight. Some steals are accessories you can't
  get anywhere else yet. For example, the **Dhorme Chimera** on the
  Grandport world map has a rare **Ring of Rage**.

## Exploring

- **Explore each new region before following the story.** Sanctums are easy
  to walk past, and every hour without a Vision is MRP you didn't earn.
- Hug the walls. Some chests are invisible until an **Inspect** prompt pops
  up, and some passages don't show on the mini-map until you walk into them.
- Talk to everyone. Some NPCs heal you for free or sell items outside of
  shops, and **bulletin boards** and townsfolk point you at side quests and
  good grinding spots.
- Do side quests as soon as they open. They pay out gear while it's still
  useful, and many give **Magicite Shards**.
- **Dialogue choices** with party members give permanent stat bonuses. The
  first is outside the Earth Shrine: your answer gives Rain +3 Strength,
  Stamina or Spirit. The wording hints at which.
- The best gear mostly comes from **chests**, not steals or drops.
  Consumables are the reverse: Ethers can't be bought, so they come from
  drops.
- Equipment isn't a straight upgrade path. Read the secondary effects.
- Check the weapon and armor shops in every new town, but compare with what
  you've found first. Chests on the way to a town often hold better weapons.
  Sell old gear if you're short.
- Which enemies you meet depends on the **ground you're on**. Grass, trees
  and paved ground in the same area can each have their own encounters.
- Some areas have a **rare, tougher enemy**. Libra describes it as powerful
  and hard to stagger, but well rewarded. **Medusa** on the Dirnado world map
  is one; rarely, you can steal a **Jeweled Ring** from it.
- Riding a **Chocobo** lowers the encounter rate, and it avoids the Antlion
  ambush on Grandport's roads. Get off and run when you want fights.
- Dungeons show a **recommended level** when you enter. A couple of levels
  under is fine. Further under, go grind or do side quests first.
- **Nothing is permanently missable.** The game warns you before a point of
  no return, and everything behind it can still be done later.

## Saving and healing

- You can **save anywhere on the world map**, plus at save points in towns
  and dungeons.
- After you've touched a dungeon's save points, you can **teleport** between
  the entrance and the deepest one you've reached.
- **Fast travel** from the Map screen to anywhere you've been. It doesn't
  work underground.
- **Tents** fully restore HP and MP and revive anyone who's down. Use one at
  a save point or on the world map.
- An **inn** does the same for 100 gil, a third of a Tent's 300. In a
  dungeon, teleport back to the entrance, fast travel to an inn, then come
  back and teleport to where you were.
- The **encounter sensor** on the mini-map turns redder as a fight gets
  close. Use it to time saves and heals.

## Difficulty and battle rewards

- Difficulty can be changed **anytime** from the menu.
- **Casual** gives enemies 60% of their HP and a much smaller stagger gauge.
  EXP, gil and drop rates are **the same as Normal**.
- **Expert** is tougher but gives **1.5× MRP**. EXP and gil don't change.
- Bonuses on the results screen:

| Bonus | How | Reward |
|-------|-----|--------|
| **No Damage** | Win without taking any damage | +20% EXP |
| **Overkill** | Finish the fight during the Bonus Phase and keep attacking | +30% gil |
| **Stagger Finish** | Defeat the last enemy while it's staggered | +30% MRP |

- For grinding, easy enemies you can farm all three bonuses from often beat
  tougher ones. Taking enemies down one at a time so the last one dies
  staggered gets you both Stagger Finish and Overkill.
- **Everyone gets EXP**, including characters on the bench.
- Running away earns nothing.
- Each enemy rolls separately for its drops: about 1 in 8 or 1 in 12 for the
  normal item, and **1 in 30** for the rare one.

### Metal Minituar

- It has only a few HP, and every hit that lands does **1 damage**. Casual
  gives it the fewest HP and doesn't lower the EXP, so switch to Casual to
  farm it.
- It resists every element. Use plain, non-elemental attacks.
- Normal attacks land less than half the time. From its fourth turn on, it
  has about a 1 in 3 chance each turn to run.
- **Multi-hit** abilities roll to hit on every hit, so they're your best bet.
  Lasswell's **Tranquility** works well. **Limit Bursts**, **Resonances** and
  an **Esper's finishing move** always hit.
- The other enemy in that spot is **Medusa**, worth over 1,000 EXP and about
  1,000 gil, so the bad rolls still pay.

### Mini Gil Snapper

- It counters physical attacks, so stagger it with **Ice magic**.
- Steal a **Mist Ether**, or rarely a **Small Golden Shell**, which sells for
  2,500 gil.

### Pots

- A Pot asks for one item. Use that item on **your own party** and the Pot
  knocks itself out, which counts as a win.
- Switch to **Expert** first. There's no risk, and it's 1.5× MRP.
- Each Pot only pays out once. If you don't have the item, run. It'll still
  be there when you come back.
- Damage does nothing to a Pot. Every turn it asks for its item again and
  heals itself to full.
- Pots sometimes drop an **Elixir**.

| Pot | Where | Wants | MRP (Normal) |
|-----|-------|-------|--------------|
| **Citrine Pot** | Siren's Tower, Mobliz Shipyard | Ether | 120 |
| **Viridian Pot** | Wind Shrine, Water Shrine (on a balcony in the room with the water level crystals) | Mist Ether | 200 |
