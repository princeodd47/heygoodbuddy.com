# Scorekeeping

**Scorekeeper – Basic** is a JudgeHub exam that's open to Level 0 judges. It
checks that you understand GEM and are ready to be both Scorekeeper and Head
Judge at an event, or to scorekeep side events at a World Tour stop. Find it
under Exams › My Exams, next to L1A and L1B.

This page collects the parts of the Tournament Rules and Policy (TRP) and the
Procedure and Penalty Guide (PPG) that a scorekeeper uses. The
[Practice Quiz](quiz.md) has a **Scorekeeping only** set for this page.

## Quick reference

| Question | Answer | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Points for a win / bye / draw / loss | 1 / 1 / 0 / 0 | TRP A.4 |
| First tiebreaker after match points | Cumulative Match Points (higher is better) | TRP A.4 |
| Games needed to win a match | 1, unless the TO announced otherwise beforehand | TRP 3.1 |
| Who reports a result | Both players agree on it, then submit it | TRP 3.4 |
| Who a player tells to drop | The Scorekeeper, or drop in GEM | TRP 3.6 |
| No-show | Not at the table 10 minutes in. Match lost, dropped unless they check in before the next pairings | TRP 3.6, PPG 3.1 |
| Both players get a Game Loss | Record a double match loss | PPG 1.4 |
| Disqualified player | Loses the current match and is dropped with no place in the standings | PPG 1.4 |
| Swiss rounds for 17–32 players | 5, then top 8 | TRP A.2 |
| Classic Constructed round | 55 minutes | TRP A.3 |

## The scorekeeper's job

### Responsibilities (TRP 2.7)

A scorekeeper collects tournament information and turns it into pairings,
standings and records. They:

- Generate the correct pairings for each round.
- Enter every round's results into GEM.
- Produce standings, usually after the last Swiss round.
- Fix any wrong tournament information **together with the Head Judge**.
- Make sure everything in GEM is true and correct before it's submitted.

Scorekeepers and judges keep talking all event: when to start the next
round, which results are still missing, and so on. At a small event the
scorekeeper may also be the TO or a judge. At a big event one dedicated
person may scorekeep several tournaments at once.

### Where the role fits (TRP 2.1, 2.4)

- The Scorekeeper is a **tournament official**, along with the TO, Head
  Judge, Floor Judges and Game Attendants. Coverage members are not.
- **Tier 1** (except Skirmish): officials may also play, and the TO may
  also judge.
- **Tier 2 and up** (and Skirmish): officials may not play, and the TO may
  not judge.
- The TO and Head Judge should stay in their roles for the whole
  tournament. Other officials can swap roles.
- **Reporting results to LSS** is the TO's job.
- Only LSS, and the TO after the event, may publish tournament information
  such as names, decklists and results. A judge or scorekeeper shouldn't
  share it on their own, even though they can see it (TRP 3.11).

### GEM (TRP 1.1, 1.5)

- GEM (Game Event Manager) runs player participation at every Flesh and
  Blood event. Tier 1 and 2 tournaments can be run by any TO with an active
  GEM organizer account. Tier 3 and 4 are run by LSS or its OP partners.
- Every player needs a GEM profile. Its **Player ID** identifies them.
- A player may have **only one** profile. Duplicates are sorted out by
  emailing op@fabtcg.com.
- Invite-only or age-limited events (Nationals, Pro Tours, Worlds) need ID.
  Proof that the player controls the GEM profile counts. So do a passport,
  driver license, government or school ID, or a birth certificate for
  under-13s.

## Rounds and matches

### Swiss and elimination (TRP 3.1)

- **Swiss rounds** pair players with similar records, using standings, match
  history and the format. Everyone plays every round.
- **Elimination rounds** (the top cut, or play-offs) knock out the loser of
  each match. GEM drops eliminated players by itself.
- The usual structure is timed Swiss, then an **untimed top 8**.
- A match is **first to 1 game** unless the TO announced otherwise before the
  tournament.
- Untimed rounds are best-of-N: the first to win a majority of N games wins.
- In timed Swiss, a drawn game with time left means the players start a new
  game. The **match** is only a draw once time runs out and the end-of-match
  procedure ends in a draw.

### How many Swiss rounds (TRP A.2)

Use this when there's no official structure for the event. If the TO runs a
different number, they must say so at registration or in the event
advertising.

| Players | Swiss rounds | Play-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 8 | 3 | None |
| 9–16 | 5 (4 for Limited with a draft play-off) | Top 4 (top 8 for Limited with a draft play-off) |
| 17–32 | 5 | Top 8 |
| 33–64 | 6 | Top 8 |
| 65–128 | 7 | Top 8 |
| 129–224 | 8 | Top 8 |
| 225–384 | 9 | Top 8 |
| 385–736 | 10 | Top 8 |

The single-day table aims for one undefeated player and every X-1 player in
the play-off.

### Round times (TRP A.3)

Times include the 5-minute start-of-game procedure. A TO who changes them
must tell players before the tournament or advertise it.

| Format | Round |
| --- | --- |
| Classic Constructed | 55 minutes |
| Blitz | 35 minutes |
| Sealed Deck | 35 minutes |
| Booster Draft | 35 minutes |
| Welcome Deck | 30 minutes |

| Limited step | Sealed | Draft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration | 20 minutes* | 15 minutes* |
| Verification | 10 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Build and preparation | 30 minutes* | 10 minutes* |

\* Add 10 minutes at World Premiere Weekend events.

### The round clock (TRP 3.2)

- Start the timer once **most** players are seated or have had a fair
  chance to sit. Players may do the start-of-game procedure early but can't
  take the first turn until the clock starts.
- A judge pause of **more than 1 minute** while the clock is running gives
  that match at least as much extra time.
- A regular deck check gives the time it took **plus 3 minutes**, but the
  match never gets more than a full round in total.
- Only time lost while the clock was running is given back.
- Feature matches get extra time for moving, or are timed separately.
- If the round starts early because everyone finished, tardiness is still
  counted from when the last round was **scheduled** to end (PPG 3.1).

## Results

### Reporting (TRP 3.4)

- At the end of a match, **both players agree on the result**, then submit
  it to the Scorekeeper.
- At time in Swiss, the turn-player finishes their turn and **one more
  turn** is played. If nobody has won, the game is a draw. The match goes
  to whoever won more games, or it's a draw.
- Elimination rounds can't end in a draw. The last step of the play-off
  procedure gives the match to the player with the **higher standing before
  the match**, so the judges need accurate standings from you.

### What to record

| What happened | Record | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intentional draw in Swiss | Draw | TRP 3.5 |
| Intentional draw in elimination | Not allowed. A judge runs the end-of-match procedure | TRP 3.5 |
| A player concedes | Loss for that player | TRP 3.5 |
| One player refuses to play | That player concedes | TRP 3.5 |
| Both players refuse to play | Loss for both | TRP 3.5 |
| A player concedes when they're not allowed to | Drop them, unless they have a real reason to stop (illness, an emergency, being uncomfortable with the opponent) | TRP 3.5 |
| One player gets a Game Loss in a first-to-1 match | Match win for the opponent | PPG 1.4 |
| Both players get a Game Loss at once | Double match loss | PPG 1.4 |
| Game Loss after time is up or between matches | It applies to the player's **next** game | PPG 1.4 |
| Improper Concession or Improperly Determining a Winner after the result is in | The result stands. The Game Loss goes on the **next** round | PPG 4.4, 4.9 |
| A player refuses a feature match at Pro, or in any play-off | Match loss. If both refuse, loss for both | TRP 3.11 |

A concession or a drop can never be traded for anything (TRP 3.5, 3.6). Once
players talk about prizes that depend on the match, they can no longer
concede.

### Byes

A bye is worth **1 match point**, the same as a win (TRP A.4). For the Match
Loss % tiebreaker, a bye round doesn't count as played. See below.

## Standings and tiebreakers

Players are ranked by these, in order (TRP A.4):

1. Higher **Match Points**
2. Higher **Cumulative Match Points** (CMP)
3. Lower **Match Loss %** (MLP)
4. Lower average **Opponent Match Loss %**
5. Higher average **Opponent CMP**
6. Random

Rule of thumb: **the earlier the loss, the worse it is for your
tiebreakers.**

### Cumulative Match Points

CMP is a weighted win rate from 0 to 1. With *R* rounds played so far, a
win in round *r* is worth 2<sup>R</sup> − 2<sup>r−1</sup>. CMP is the
points from the rounds you won divided by the points from every round.
Early rounds are worth the most.

| After 3 rounds | Round 1 (worth 7) | Round 2 (worth 6) | Round 3 (worth 4) | CMP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W W L | 7 | 6 | — | 13/17 = 0.76 |
| W L W | 7 | — | 4 | 11/17 = 0.65 |
| L W W | — | 6 | 4 | 10/17 = 0.59 |

All three players have 2 match points. The one who lost last ranks highest.
Winning every round gives a CMP of 1. Winning none gives 0.

### Match Loss %

MLP is losses divided by **rounds played**. A bye round isn't played. A
draw is played but isn't a loss.

| Record | Rounds played | Losses | MLP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W D L | 3 | 1 | 0.33 |
| W B L | 2 | 1 | 0.50 |
| W W W | 3 | 0 | 0 |

So for a player with any losses, a **bye is worse than a draw**. A loss is
always worse than a draw.

### What the tiebreakers explain

Because a draw and a loss are both 0 points, players can be tempted to turn
a draw into someone's win. That's why the concession rules are strict and
why Improperly Determining a Winner and Improper Concession move the Game
Loss to the next round. A draw is still better than a loss for the player:
it keeps their MLP lower.

## Drops and no-shows

- A player can drop **any time** by telling the Scorekeeper (TRP 3.6). At big events
  they should drop in GEM.
- Only the **Head Judge** can put a dropped player back in. Re-entry in the
  same round is usually fine. Later re-entry can cause problems that aren't
  obvious, so the HJ decides.
- A Disqualification is a drop too. The player loses their current match
  and gets **no place in the standings**. Everyone below moves up one,
  unless the cut has already been made (PPG 1.4).

| When the player drops | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Before the first pairings | Treated as never having played. Not in the final standings |
| After a round's pairings are up | They concede that round's match, then drop |
| Not at their table 10 minutes into the round | They lose the match (Game Loss for Tardiness). Drop them before the next pairings unless they check in with the Scorekeeper first |
| Both players over 10 minutes late | Both get a Game Loss (PPG 3.1) |
| Under 10 minutes late | Tardy, not dropped |
| They told the Scorekeeper why they'll miss the round | They don't have to be dropped |
| After making the top cut | They concede their first top-cut match and keep the prizes for their place. **Nobody replaces them** in the cut |

- Players who drop early may lose participation prizes, at the TO's call.
- A player dropping mid-draft keeps their drafted cards, the pack they're
  holding and their unopened packs. A judge oversees it.

## Decklists

| REL | Decklists required (TRP A.5) |
| --- | --- |
| Professional | Yes |
| Competitive | Yes |
| Casual | No, unless the TO asks. Never for Welcome Deck |

- Constructed decklists should be submitted **in GEM** where possible (TRP 3.7). The
  TO may require it.
- Constructed lists are due **before round 1**. Limited lists are due at the
  end of deck construction.
- A player without a required decklist can't start their match and counts
  as **tardy** until the TO has it (PPG 3.1).
- Decklists stay private to the player and officials. At Pro REL,
  Constructed play-off players get copies of their opponent's list. Limited
  play-offs don't.

## Limited and team events

### Draft pods in GEM

When an event drafts again partway through, such as a second draft or a
top 8 draft:

1. Enter every result for the round and click **Accept All Reports**.
2. Click **Back to Tournament**.
3. Under **Tournament Rounds**, open the cog. Set the format to Booster
   Draft and tick **New Draft Pods**.
4. For more Swiss rounds, untick **Cut to Top 8** and pair the next round.
   For a top 8 draft, tick **Cut to Top 8** and **Elimination Rounds**
   instead.
5. Hide the pairings until the draft starts, so players can't scout their
   opponents.
6. **Print Draft Pods** gives the seating for each pod.

The same cog menu changes the format, for example when the top 8 is
Constructed after Limited Swiss rounds.

### Team events (TRP 9.2)

- A team has **three players**, assigned positions A, B and C at
  registration. Positions can't be swapped. Each player plays the opposing
  player in the same position.
- The first team to win **two games** wins the match. A game still going
  ends right away.
- At time, games in progress are finished. The team with more game wins
  takes the match. Otherwise it's a draw.
- One member can't play (dropped or disqualified): they forfeit their game
  and the other two still play.
- Two or more members can't play: the team forfeits the match and should be
  dropped.
- In Team Booster Draft, each round's pod is the two teams playing each
  other. Clockwise seating is A1, B2, C1, A2, B1, C2.

### Ultimate Pit Fight (TRP 9.3)

Multiplayer events may rank players by the order they lost. The second place
player is the one who lost third in a four-player game.
